# Chapter 7: Managing Human Resources and Labour Relations

## 7.1 What Is Human Resources?

- **Human resource (HR) management** is the process of hiring, developing, motivating, and evaluating employees to achieve organizational goals. HR decisions follow from the firm’s business model, with the aim of making employees a source of competitive advantage.
- The HR process runs in order: job analysis and design → HR planning and forecasting → recruitment → selection → training and development → performance planning and evaluation → compensation and benefits. It is sequential because people can’t be trained or paid until they are selected and placed.
- A **job analysis** studies the tasks needed to do a job well. Its results feed the **job description** (the job’s tasks and responsibilities) and the **job specification** (the skills, knowledge, and abilities a person needs to fill it). Table 7.1 shows both for a college recruiter.
- An **HR demand forecast** estimates how many people the firm will need at a future time. An **internal supply forecast** estimates how many current employees will be available to fill those jobs. Alcon Labs had to decide whether a new product needed more sales reps.
- **Succession planning** reviews managers’ performance to find people who can be promoted into vacancies; Best Buy used it to cut turnover, and its store general managers average five years in the job. A **contingent worker** wants to work, but not on a permanent, continuous basis (for example, extra help for the holidays).

## 7.2 Employee Recruitment

- Applicants come from the **internal labour market** (current employees, often found through an HR information system; promotions and transfers are the usual result) or the **external labour market** (people outside the firm).
- **Recruitment** is attracting qualified people to form an applicant pool. Methods include print, radio, web, and TV ads; **job fairs** (one- or two-day corporate open houses); college recruiting; and online job boards like Indeed and Glassdoor, where software scans résumés for keywords.
- Referrals and social networks matter, especially for managerial and professional roles. LinkedIn is the most popular professional network, with more than 930 million members, and rests on the idea of no more than six degrees of separation. It also raises privacy questions.
- **Recruitment branding** presents an accurate and positive image of the firm to recruits. A **realistic job preview** tells candidates the realities of the job and company (standards, promotion chances, culture) so they can judge fit.

## 7.3 Employee Selection

- **Selection** determines which applicants have the qualifications to succeed. In the **successive hurdles approach**, an applicant can be rejected at any step; clearing every hurdle usually leads to an offer.
- Steps: initial screening (application or résumé and a brief interview of 30 minutes or less) → employment testing (Wonderlic tests of cognitive ability, motivation potential, and knowledge and skills; personality assessments like Yankee Candle’s) → selection interview → background and reference checks → physical exam and drug testing → decision to hire.
- The **selection interview** is the most widely used hiring tool. It probes experience, skills, education, and career interests, and may pose realistic job situations. W.L. Gore listens for answers that reveal poor teamwork or complaining.
- The decision to hire is nearly always made by the new employee’s manager, and the offer may depend on passing a physical or drug test.
- Firms should treat applicants like customers. Ritz-Carlton greets applicants with a “warm welcome” and “fond farewell,” sends every applicant a thank-you note, and gives new hires seven days of training. Its motto: “We Are Ladies and Gentlemen Serving Ladies and Gentlemen.”

## 7.4 Employee Training & Development

- **Training and development** are learning situations in which employees gain knowledge or skills that improve job performance. Training happens on the job or off the job.
- **Orientation** starts new-employee training: a formal program (often a half-day class on company history, values, policies, customers, and products), then a more informal job orientation by the supervisor that may last days or weeks.
- On-the-job methods: job instruction, **coaching** (guidance from experienced employees), special projects, **job rotation** (moving through several jobs over time, like Walmart’s management trainees), **apprenticeship** (job plus classroom training for up to four years in skilled trades), and **mentoring** (a senior person shares job and career advice; inexpensive, with instant feedback, and possible at a distance as at Dow Chemical).
- Off-the-job methods: classroom work (cases, role-play, lectures), e-learning, **programmed instruction** (online, self-paced, highly structured modules, used by Union Pacific), and **simulation** (such as American Airlines’ flight simulator, which transfers learning directly to the job).
- International assignments add challenges for expatriates: schools, housing, medical care, banking, transportation, government forms, and community life. KPMG has every employee and partner affirm its Code of Conduct each year.

## 7.5 Performance Planning & Evaluation

- A **performance appraisal** compares actual performance with expected performance to measure an employee’s contribution and to guide decisions on training, pay, promotion, and job changes.
- The process: the manager sets standards → the employee works to meet them → the supervisor evaluates output quality and quantity plus job knowledge, initiative, relationships, attendance, and punctuality → reward or job-change decisions follow. Unsatisfactory work can lead to a **performance improvement plan** listing what must improve, by when, and what happens if it doesn’t.
- Rewards are positive feedback that reinforce good performance. Most companies have moved from annual reviews toward continuous, real-time feedback.
- Appraisal information comes from rating scales (such as a behaviour-based scale where 9 is outstanding and 1 is unacceptable), supervisor logs of job incidents, and sales or production statistics.

## 7.6 Employee Compensation & Benefits

- **Compensation** includes pay and benefits and is closely tied to appraisals. Internal influences: pay rises with skills, experience, and job level, and jobs of equal importance get similar pay (two machine operators both at \$21/hour). External influences: competitors’ wages. Firms can pay at, above, or below the going rate.
- **Direct pay** is wages or salary; **indirect pay** is benefits and services. **Base pay** (hourly rate or salary) doesn’t depend on output; commissions and incentive pay can be added.
- Accelerated commission example: \$1,000 base, plus 3% on the first \$50,000 of sales, 4% on the next \$30,000, and 5% on sales beyond \$80,000. At \$160,000 in sales: \$1,000 + \$1,500 + \$1,200 + \$4,000 = **\$7,700**.
- **Bonuses** reward reaching specific goals. **Profit-sharing** pays employees part of the firm’s profit, usually once a year; Choice Homes gives everyone the same share.
- Some indirect pay is required by law (the text’s U.S. examples: unemployment compensation, workers’ compensation, Social Security). Optional benefits include paid time off, health, dental, and vision insurance, retirement savings, pensions, and stock options. Some firms let employees mix and match benefits.
- Starbucks gave part-time workers health benefits and stock options. Howard Schultz made 220 pitches to raise \$2.8 million and was turned down in all but 12. Turnover fell to 15% for store managers and 65% for part-timers, against 200–300% at McDonald’s and Taco Bell.

## 7.7 The Labour Relations Process

- A **labour union** represents workers in dealing with management over wages, hours, and working conditions. The labour relations process has three phases: union organizing, negotiating a labour agreement (**collective bargaining**), and administering the agreement, mainly by handling grievances.
- The labour movement has local unions (about 60,000), national and international unions (about 75), and two federations. U.S. membership fell from 20.1% of workers (17.7 million) in 1983 to 10.7% (14.8 million) in 2017.
- A **local union** represents workers at a plant or in an area. Its three main functions are collective bargaining (every three or four years), worker relations and membership services, and community and political activities. A **shop steward** is an elected union official who represents members to management when workers have complaints.
- National unions range from a few thousand members to over a million (Teamsters); their number has fallen mostly through mergers (PACE, 1999).
- Organizing: an organizer asks workers to sign **authorization cards** showing interest in union representation. Employers may speak against unions, but it is illegal to interfere directly with card signing or coerce employees.
- Bargaining teams exchange proposals and counterproposals over several rounds. The contract must be approved by top management and ratified by members; it is then legally binding and covers union security, management rights, wages, benefits, and job security.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Human resource management** | The process of hiring, developing, motivating, and evaluating employees to achieve organizational goals. |
| **Job analysis** | A study of the tasks required to do a job well. |
| **Job description** | A list of the tasks and responsibilities of a job. |
| **Job specification** | The skills, knowledge, and abilities a person must have to fill a job. |
| **HR demand forecast** | An estimate of the number of people the organization will need at a future time. |
| **Internal supply forecast** | An estimate of how many current employees will be available to fill jobs at a future time. |
| **Succession planning** | Reviewing managers’ performance to identify people who can fill vacancies and be promoted. |
| **Contingent worker** | Someone who wants to work, but not on a permanent, continuous basis. |
| **Internal labour market** | Employees currently employed by the firm, a source of applicants through promotions and transfers. |
| **External labour market** | The pool of potential applicants outside the firm. |
| **Recruitment** | The process of attracting qualified people to form an applicant pool. |
| **Job fair** | A one- or two-day event where applicants learn about job openings, tour the firm, and are encouraged to apply. |
| **Recruitment branding** | Presenting an accurate and positive image of the firm to those being recruited. |
| **Realistic job preview** | Information that tells candidates the realities of the job and the firm so they can judge it accurately. |
| **Selection** | Determining which applicants have the qualifications needed to succeed on the job. |
| **Successive hurdles approach** | A selection process in which an applicant can be rejected at any step and must clear every step to get an offer. |
| **Selection interview** | An in-depth discussion of an applicant’s experience, skills, education, and career interests; the most widely used hiring tool. |
| **Training and development** | Learning situations in which employees gain knowledge or skills to improve job performance. |
| **Orientation** | Getting a new employee ready to perform, through a formal company program and a job briefing from the supervisor. |
| **Coaching** | Guidance given to new employees by experienced ones. |
| **Job rotation** | Reassigning workers to several different jobs over time. |
| **Apprenticeship** | On-the-job instruction combined with classroom training, lasting up to four years, common in the skilled trades. |
| **Mentoring** | A senior manager or experienced employee giving job- and career-related information to a mentee. |
| **Programmed instruction** | An online, self-paced, highly structured training method that presents concepts and problems in modules. |
| **Simulation** | Training on a scaled-down or mock version of the real task, such as a flight simulator. |
| **Performance appraisal** | Comparing actual with expected performance to judge an employee’s contribution and guide pay, training, and promotion decisions. |
| **Performance improvement plan** | A plan listing the behaviours to improve, the milestones and time frame, and what happens if performance doesn’t improve. |
| **Direct pay** | The wage or salary an employee receives. |
| **Indirect pay** | Employee benefits and services, such as pensions, health insurance, and vacation time. |
| **Base pay** | An hourly rate or salary received regardless of output level. |
| **Profit-sharing plan** | A plan that pays employees a portion of the firm’s profit, usually once a year. |
| **Labour union** | An organization that represents workers in dealing with management over wages, hours, and working conditions. |
| **Collective bargaining** | Negotiating a labour agreement on pay and working arrangements acceptable to both union and management. |
| **Shop steward** | An elected union official who represents members to management when workers have complaints. |
| **Authorization cards** | Cards workers sign to show they want a union to represent them. |

### Multiple choice

**1. Which list puts the first four steps of the HR process in the correct order?**

A. Job analysis and design, HR planning, recruitment, selection  
B. HR planning, selection, recruitment, job analysis  
C. Selection, recruitment, training, job analysis  
D. Recruitment, job analysis, selection, HR planning  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Job analysis and design, HR planning, recruitment, selection.** The process is sequential: you can’t recruit before you know what jobs you need, or train before you select.

**2. A document listing the tasks and responsibilities of a job is a:**

A. Job specification  
B. Performance appraisal  
C. Job analysis  
D. Job description  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Job description.** The job specification lists the skills and qualifications a person needs instead.

**3. “Bachelor’s degree in HR, two years’ experience, proficient in Excel” belongs in the:**

A. Realistic job preview  
B. Performance improvement plan  
C. Job specification  
D. Job description  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Job specification.** Qualifications a person must have go in the job specification.

**4. Estimating how many current employees will be available to fill jobs next year is a(n):**

A. Job analysis  
B. Succession plan  
C. HR demand forecast  
D. Internal supply forecast  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Internal supply forecast.** The demand forecast estimates how many people will be needed in total.

**5. Best Buy reviews managers’ performance to identify who can be promoted into vacancies. This is:**

A. Recruitment branding  
B. Collective bargaining  
C. Succession planning  
D. Job rotation  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Succession planning.** Succession planning prepares people to fill future vacancies.

**6. A store hires an experienced contractor only for the holiday season. That person is a:**

A. Shop steward  
B. Mentor  
C. Contingent worker  
D. Apprentice  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Contingent worker.** Contingent workers want to work, but not on a permanent, continuous basis.

**7. Promotions and job transfers are the most common results of:**

A. College recruiting  
B. Job fairs  
C. Internal recruiting  
D. External recruiting  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Internal recruiting.** They draw on the internal labour market.

**8. Recruitment branding means:**

A. Recruiting only from rival brands  
B. Putting the company logo on job ads  
C. Presenting an accurate and positive image of the firm to recruits  
D. Paying recruiters a commission per hire  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Presenting an accurate and positive image of the firm to recruits.** It often includes a realistic job preview.

**9. In the successive hurdles approach to selection:**

A. Applicants choose which steps to complete  
B. An applicant can be rejected at any step, and clearing all steps usually leads to an offer  
C. Only the final interview matters  
D. Every applicant gets an interview  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. An applicant can be rejected at any step, and clearing all steps usually leads to an offer.** Each step is a hurdle the applicant must clear.

**10. What is the most widely used tool for making hiring decisions?**

A. Drug testing  
B. The selection interview  
C. Wonderlic tests  
D. Reference checks  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. The selection interview.** It assesses experience, skills, communication, and motivation in depth.

**11. Wonderlic pre-employment tests assess:**

A. Cognitive ability, motivation potential, and knowledge and skills  
B. Credit history and legal record  
C. Physical fitness  
D. Only typing speed  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Cognitive ability, motivation potential, and knowledge and skills.** The suite can be used for each phase of hiring.

**12. Who nearly always makes the final decision to hire?**

A. The CEO  
B. The union steward  
C. The manager the new employee will report to  
D. The HR department  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. The manager the new employee will report to.** The offer may still depend on a physical exam or drug test.

**13. Walmart management trainees spend time in merchandising, customer service, credit, and HR. This is:**

A. Apprenticeship  
B. Programmed instruction  
C. Simulation  
D. Job rotation  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Job rotation.** Job rotation reassigns workers to several jobs over time.

**14. Training that combines on-the-job instruction and classroom work for up to four years in the skilled trades is:**

A. An apprenticeship  
B. Mentoring  
C. Orientation  
D. Coaching  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. An apprenticeship.** Carpentry, plumbing, and electrical work commonly use apprenticeships.

**15. American Airlines pilots practise hazardous manoeuvres in a mock cockpit. This is:**

A. Programmed instruction  
B. Mentoring  
C. Job rotation  
D. Simulation  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Simulation.** Simulation allows a direct transfer of learning to the job without risk.

**16. Union Pacific’s self-paced, highly structured online training modules are an example of:**

A. Programmed instruction  
B. Apprenticeship  
C. A job fair  
D. Coaching  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Programmed instruction.** It suits a workforce spread across a large area and saves travel costs.

**17. An employee whose work is unsatisfactory may be placed on a:**

A. Performance improvement plan  
B. Succession plan  
C. Profit-sharing plan  
D. Realistic job preview  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Performance improvement plan.** It sets out what must improve, by when, and the consequences.

**18. A salesperson earns \$1,000 base pay plus 3% on the first \$50,000 of sales, 4% on the next \$30,000, and 5% on sales above \$80,000. What is the pay on \$160,000 of sales?**

A. \$9,000  
B. \$6,700  
C. \$7,700  
D. \$8,000  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. \$7,700.** \$1,000 + \$1,500 (3% × 50,000) + \$1,200 (4% × 30,000) + \$4,000 (5% × 80,000) = \$7,700.

**19. Pensions, health insurance, and vacation time are examples of:**

A. Direct pay  
B. Commission  
C. Base pay  
D. Indirect pay  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Indirect pay.** Direct pay is the wage or salary itself.

**20. Which is NOT one of the three phases of the labour relations process?**

A. Administering the agreement  
B. Recruiting union leaders from management  
C. Union organizing  
D. Negotiating the labour agreement  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Recruiting union leaders from management.** Administering the agreement is mostly handling grievances.

**21. An elected union official who represents members to management when workers have complaints is a:**

A. Broker  
B. Contingent worker  
C. Mentor  
D. Shop steward  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Shop steward.** For most members, local officials like stewards are their main union contact.

**22. Before a negotiated union contract becomes legally binding, it must be:**

A. Approved by top management and ratified by union members  
B. Approved by a job fair  
C. Signed by the federal government  
D. Reviewed by competitors  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Approved by top management and ratified by union members.** Once both sides approve, it covers wages, benefits, job security, and more.

**23. During a union organizing campaign, it is illegal for an employer to:**

A. Speak out against unions in letters  
B. Put up posters about its position  
C. Directly interfere with card signing or coerce employees  
D. Hold employee assemblies  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Directly interfere with card signing or coerce employees.** Employers commonly resist, but they can’t interfere directly or coerce.

**24. Investors doubted Howard Schultz’s plan to give part-time Starbucks staff benefits. What happened?**

A. Part-timers declined the benefits  
B. Turnover rose to 300%  
C. Starbucks went bankrupt  
D. Turnover dropped well below rivals’ levels  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Turnover dropped well below rivals’ levels.** Part-time turnover was 65%, versus 200–300% at McDonald’s and Taco Bell.

### True or false

**1. The HR process is sequential because employees can’t be trained or paid until they are selected.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** Each step depends on the one before.

**2. A job specification lists a job’s tasks and responsibilities.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** That’s the job description; the specification lists required skills and abilities.

**3. The external labour market consists of the firm’s current employees.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** Current employees make up the internal labour market.

**4. LinkedIn is based on the idea that any two people are linked by no more than six degrees of separation.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** It has more than 930 million members.

**5. The initial screening interview is usually 30 minutes or less.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** It follows the application form or résumé.

**6. The decision to hire is usually made by the HR department.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** It is nearly always made by the new employee’s manager.

**7. Mentoring is expensive and gives slow feedback.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** It’s inexpensive and gives instantaneous feedback.

**8. Most companies have moved away from annual-only reviews toward continuous, real-time feedback.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** This speeds up skill development.

**9. On the textbook’s behaviour-based rating scale, a 1 means outstanding performance.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** 9 is outstanding; 1 is very poor to unacceptable.

**10. Jobs of equal importance to the firm are usually paid at similar rates.**

    **True.** The text’s example is two machine operators both paid \$21 an hour.
