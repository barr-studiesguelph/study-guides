# Chapter 8: Operations Management

!!! abstract "Chapter overview"
    How manufacturers and service firms turn inputs into outputs: types of production, technology, capacity and location planning, scheduling with Gantt and PERT charts, and total quality management.

!!! tip "Practise interactively"

## 8.1 Defining Operations Management

- Every firm must find the most efficient and effective way to produce its goods or services. Customers expect high quality, reasonable prices, and timely delivery, so manufacturers automate, improve processes, focus on quality control, and work closely with suppliers. Service firms use tools like mobile banking and online courses.
- Operations management creates four types of **utility** for customers: **time** (available when needed), **place** (convenient location), **ownership or possession** (the benefit of owning it), and **form** (raw materials transformed into a finished good).

## 8.2 Manufacturing Process

- **Operations management** covers all the activities that transform a product idea into a finished product, including planning and controlling the systems that produce goods and services. The upper-level manager who directs this is the **operations manager**.
- The transformation process turns inputs (materials, labour, money, information) into outputs (goods and services).
- **Due diligence** is the action directors or managers take to make sure potential risks and errors are mitigated.
- Manufacturers must keep improving efficiency: focus on quality, hold down material and labour costs, and eliminate costs that add no value to the finished product.

## 8.3 Service Processes

- Canada has shifted from goods to services. About 10% of Canadian workers were in manufacturing in 2017, down from 30% in 1950, and services account for about 80% of jobs.
- Three differences between manufacturing and services: **intangibility** (services can’t be touched), **customization** (goods are mostly standardized; services are often tailored, like a haircut), and **customer contact** (many services are bought and consumed at the same time).
- Burger King operates in 100 countries, serves over 11 million customers a day, and bought Tim Hortons in December 2014 for \$12 billion, becoming the third-largest fast-food chain. It moved its headquarters to Canada to lower its corporate taxes.

## 8.4 Types of Operation Processes

- **Make-to-order**: goods are customized to each buyer’s specifications; low volume, high variety, and longer cycles (print and sign shops).
- **Mass production** (make-to-stock): high volumes of identical goods made in advance based on forecasts and held in inventory. Cycles are shorter, and it benefits from **economies of scale**, the lower cost per unit that comes from producing more units.
- **Mass customization** combines customization with mass-production efficiency, often by mass-producing up to a cut-off point and then customizing (NikeiD, Levi’s online fitting, custom M&M’s). It doesn’t suit everything; few people want custom detergent.
- **Just-in-time (JIT)** production has materials arrive just as they are needed, cutting inventory holding costs. It requires close communication and cooperation with suppliers. Print-on-demand textbooks are an example.
- **Computer-aided design (CAD)** lets engineers design and test digitally; PowerSki made 400+ design changes this way. **Computer-aided manufacturing (CAM)** determines the production steps and instructs the machines, much like a 3-D printer. **Computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM)** extends CAD/CAM to order entry, inventory, warehousing, and shipping, and controls industrial robots.

## 8.5 Operations Planning & Control

- **Capacity** is the maximum a facility can produce in a given time under normal conditions. Too little capacity loses sales and customers; too much wastes resources and inflates costs. **Continuous review** of processes helps keep them balanced.
- Services can’t be inventoried (no stored haircuts or roller-coaster rides), so they must build capacity to serve customers as demanded, considering how many customers come, when, how long each takes, and external factors like weather.
- **Material requirements planning (MRP)** software turns the sales forecast into a **master production schedule**, then uses each item’s **bill of materials** (its list of parts) and ordering lead times to work out what parts to order and when.
- Location factors: availability and cost of inputs (shipping can be up to 25% of a manufacturer’s total cost, so firms with bulky materials locate near suppliers; labour cost and unionization matter), marketing factors (nearness to customers), the existing manufacturing environment, local incentives such as tax breaks (Amazon’s HQ2 in Arlington, Virginia), and international options (lower labour costs, closer to new markets).
- Facility layout: the three main types are process, product, and fixed-position. Service layouts focus on customer behaviour. As prime land rose from \$100,000 to over \$1 million an acre, Burger King shrank restaurants from 4,000 to as little as 1,000 square feet (fewer seats, paper wrappers instead of boxes), opening up airports, food courts, and schools.

## 8.6 Operations Scheduling

- A **Gantt chart** is a simple graphical tool that shows a project’s status over time. It works best when the process is fairly simple and activities aren’t interrelated. In the Toronto Teddy Bear example, clothes and accessories are done by day 6, but sewing and stuffing don’t finish until the end of day 8.
- A **PERT chart** (Program Evaluation and Review Technique) diagrams the activities, the time each takes, and the most efficient sequence. It identifies the **critical path**: the sequence of activities that takes the most time.
- For one hiker bear, the critical path is cutting → stuffing → dressing → packaging → shipping, which takes 65 minutes. Only saving time on the critical path finishes the job sooner; speeding up other paths just makes parts wait.

## 8.7 Total Quality Management

- **Total quality management (TQM)**, or quality assurance, covers every step a company takes to make sure its goods or services meet customers’ needs. It rests on three principles: customer satisfaction, employee involvement, and continuous improvement.
- Customer satisfaction: customers define quality, and firms track satisfaction with surveys over time.
- Employee involvement: quality is everyone’s job, not just the quality control department’s. Employees are trained to spot and fix problems, often in **quality circles**, teams of people doing similar jobs who identify problems and propose solutions.
- **Continuous improvement** is a commitment to constantly improving the design, production, and delivery of goods and services.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Operations management** | All the activities involved in transforming a product idea into a finished product, including planning and controlling production systems. |
| **Operations manager** | The upper-level manager who directs the transformation of inputs into outputs. |
| **Due diligence** | Actions taken by directors or managers to ensure potential risks and errors are mitigated. |
| **Time utility** | Making a product or service available when the customer wants it. |
| **Place utility** | Making a product or service available in a convenient location. |
| **Ownership (possession) utility** | The benefit customers get from owning a product. |
| **Form utility** | The value created by transforming raw materials into a finished good. |
| **Make-to-order** | Producing low-volume, high-variety goods to each customer’s specifications. |
| **Mass production** | Producing high volumes of identical goods in advance of demand and holding them in inventory; also called make-to-stock. |
| **Economies of scale** | The lower cost per unit that results from producing a larger total number of units. |
| **Mass customization** | Combining the advantages of customized products with the efficiency of mass production. |
| **Just-in-time (JIT) production** | Arranging for materials to arrive just in time to enter production, cutting inventory holding costs. |
| **Computer-aided design (CAD)** | Software for designing and digitally testing a product before building a prototype. |
| **Computer-aided manufacturing (CAM)** | Software that determines the steps to produce a component and instructs the machines that do the work. |
| **Computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM)** | Systems that integrate design and production with order entry, inventory, warehousing, and shipping. |
| **Industrial robots** | Computer-controlled machines that perform repetitive tasks that are hard or dangerous for people. |
| **Capacity** | The maximum number of goods a facility can produce over a given time under normal working conditions. |
| **Continuous review** | Routinely reviewing processes to find improvements that increase efficiency. |
| **Material requirements planning (MRP)** | Software that uses sales forecasts and lead times to calculate how many of each part are needed and when to order them. |
| **Master production schedule** | The detailed production plan created from the sales forecast, which MRP expands into parts needs. |
| **Bill of materials** | A list of the parts that make up a finished product. |
| **Gantt chart** | A graphical tool showing the scheduled timing and status of a project’s activities. |
| **PERT chart** | A diagram of a process’s activities, their times, and the most efficient sequence. |
| **Critical path** | The sequence of activities in a process that takes the greatest amount of time. |
| **Total quality management (TQM)** | All the steps a company takes to make sure its goods or services meet customers’ needs. |
| **Quality circles** | Teams of employees doing similar jobs who identify problems and propose solutions. |
| **Continuous improvement** | The commitment to constantly improving the design, production, and delivery of goods and services. |

## Practice questions

Pick your answer before opening each one.

### Multiple choice

**1. Making a product available exactly when the customer needs it creates:**

A. Ownership utility  
B. Time utility  
C. Place utility  
D. Form utility  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Time utility.** Scheduling and the supply chain deliver time utility.

**2. Transforming raw materials into a finished good creates:**

A. Form utility  
B. Ownership utility  
C. Place utility  
D. Time utility  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Form utility.** Form utility comes from the transformation itself.

**3. Choosing convenient store and office locations for customers creates:**

A. Place utility  
B. Time utility  
C. Form utility  
D. Possession utility  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Place utility.** Place utility is about convenient access.

**4. Actions managers take to make sure potential risks and errors are mitigated are called:**

A. Due process  
B. Continuous review  
C. Due diligence  
D. Benchmarking  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Due diligence.** Operations management must apply due diligence because of its complexity.

**5. About what share of Canadian workers were employed in manufacturing in 2017?**

A. 10%  
B. 50%  
C. 80%  
D. 30%  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. 10%.** It was 30% in 1950; services now account for about 80% of jobs.

**6. Which is NOT one of the three differences between manufacturing and service operations?**

A. Customization  
B. Customer contact  
C. Services always cost less to produce  
D. Intangibility  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Services always cost less to produce.** The text lists intangibility, customization, and customer contact.

**7. A sign shop producing low-volume, high-variety jobs to each customer’s specifications uses:**

A. Mass production  
B. Make-to-order  
C. Just-in-time inventory only  
D. Mass customization  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Make-to-order.** Make-to-order usually means longer production and delivery cycles.

**8. Producing large volumes of identical goods based on forecasts and storing them for later sale is:**

A. Make-to-order  
B. Mass production  
C. Fixed-position layout  
D. Mass customization  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Mass production.** Also called a make-to-stock strategy.

**9. Economies of scale refers to:**

A. Lower cost per unit as total units produced increase  
B. Making one-of-a-kind goods  
C. Higher prices for larger products  
D. Producing goods in many countries  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Lower cost per unit as total units produced increase.** Mass production takes advantage of economies of scale.

**10. Letting customers design their own shoes through NikeiD is an example of:**

A. Mass production  
B. A Gantt chart  
C. Mass customization  
D. Make-to-order craft production  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Mass customization.** It combines customization with efficient production.

**11. What does just-in-time production depend on most?**

A. Make-to-order craft workers  
B. Very low-quality materials  
C. Close communication and cooperation with suppliers  
D. Large warehouses of spare parts  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Close communication and cooperation with suppliers.** Suppliers must deliver the right materials, at the right quality, at exactly the right time.

**12. The main cost saving from JIT is:**

A. Lower labour wages  
B. No need for suppliers  
C. Higher selling prices  
D. Lower costs of holding inventory  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Lower costs of holding inventory.** Parts don’t sit unused for long periods.

**13. Software that determines the steps to make a component and instructs the machines is:**

A. Computer-aided design (CAD)  
B. PERT  
C. Material requirements planning (MRP)  
D. Computer-aided manufacturing (CAM)  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Computer-aided manufacturing (CAM).** CAD and CAM programs can “talk” to each other.

**14. Which system also handles order entry, inventory control, warehousing, and shipping?**

A. CAD  
B. Computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM)  
C. A Gantt chart  
D. CAM  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM).** CIM expands CAD/CAM to integrate all operations.

**15. Setting production capacity too high leads to:**

A. Shorter critical paths  
B. Higher quality  
C. Lost sales and customers  
D. Wasted resources and inflated operating costs  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Wasted resources and inflated operating costs.** Too low capacity loses sales; too high wastes resources.

**16. Why is capacity planning different for services?**

A. Services can’t be stored, so capacity must meet demand as it happens  
B. Services are always made to stock  
C. Services don’t need forecasts  
D. Services have unlimited capacity  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Services can’t be stored, so capacity must meet demand as it happens.** You can’t inventory haircuts or roller-coaster rides.

**17. MRP uses which document to turn a production schedule into a list of parts needed?**

A. The Gantt chart  
B. The bill of materials  
C. The income statement  
D. The job description  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. The bill of materials.** A bill of materials lists the parts in the end product.

**18. Shipping raw materials and finished goods can be as much as what share of a manufacturer’s total cost?**

A. 75%  
B. 25%  
C. 5%  
D. 50%  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. 25%.** That’s why location decisions matter so much.

**19. Why did Burger King shrink restaurants from 4,000 to as little as 1,000 square feet?**

A. Health regulations required it  
B. Prime land became far more expensive, so smaller sites opened new locations  
C. It switched to make-to-order production  
D. Customers stopped using drive-throughs  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Prime land became far more expensive, so smaller sites opened new locations.** Land rose from about \$100,000 to over \$1 million an acre.

**20. The critical path in a PERT chart is:**

A. Any path with a quality problem  
B. The first activity in the process  
C. The cheapest sequence of activities  
D. The sequence of activities that takes the most time  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. The sequence of activities that takes the most time.** Delays on the critical path delay the whole job.

**21. To produce a teddy bear faster, Toronto Teddy Bear must save time on:**

A. Sewing the clothes  
B. Embroidering the T-shirt  
C. Making the accessories  
D. Activities on the critical path (cutting, stuffing, dressing, packaging, shipping)  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Activities on the critical path (cutting, stuffing, dressing, packaging, shipping).** Saving time elsewhere just makes those parts wait at the dressing station.

**22. Gantt charts work best when:**

A. The process is fairly simple and activities aren’t interrelated  
B. Only services are produced  
C. Hundreds of activities depend on each other  
D. There is no schedule  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. The process is fairly simple and activities aren’t interrelated.** PERT charts suit more complex schedules.

**23. Teams of employees in similar jobs who identify quality problems and propose solutions are:**

A. Focus groups  
B. Benchmarking consortiums  
C. Quality circles  
D. Labour unions  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Quality circles.** They work with management to carry out recommendations.

**24. Which is NOT one of the three TQM principles?**

A. Employee involvement  
B. Customer satisfaction  
C. Continuous improvement  
D. Minimizing employee involvement  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Minimizing employee involvement.** TQM needs everyone involved.

**25. Under TQM, who defines quality?**

A. The quality control department  
B. Government regulators  
C. Suppliers  
D. Customers  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Customers.** Firms identify the features customers want and offer them.
