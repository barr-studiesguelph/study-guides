# Chapter 10: Understanding Marketing

!!! abstract "Chapter overview"
    The marketing concept and the strategic marketing process: situation analysis and consumer behaviour, objectives and the Ansoff Matrix, segmentation, targeting and positioning, and the 4 P’s across the product life cycle.

!!! tip "Practise interactively"

## 10.1 What Is Marketing?

- The American Marketing Association defines marketing as “the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large.”
- Marketing is more than advertising and selling. It covers defining a product’s features and benefits, setting its price, identifying the target market, building awareness, getting people to buy, delivering it, and managing relationships afterward.
- Marketing is a team effort: in a movie theatre, the ticket seller and the projectionist help satisfy customers too. Most firms still have a marketing department to plan, organize, lead, and control marketing.
- The **marketing concept** means satisfying customer needs while meeting organizational goals such as profit and growth. Start by finding out what customers need, developing products to meet those needs, and engaging the whole organization. Medtronic, with over half the cardiac-device market in 2005, demanded zero defects.

## 10.2 The Strategic Marketing Process

- **Strategic planning** is a usually long-term process that helps an organization allocate resources to capitalize on market opportunities.
- The strategic marketing process has four steps: (1) conduct a situation analysis, (2) set marketing objectives, (3) develop a marketing strategy, (4) implement marketing tactics. This course covers steps 1–3.

## 10.3 Step 1: Conducting a Situation Analysis

- A **situation analysis** examines the internal environment (financial and technological resources, people, past strategies), the external environment, and consumer behaviour. PepsiCo’s “mega” brands (over \$1 billion in sales each) are a strength; Tropicana’s \$35 million packaging redesign flopped and was reversed.
- The external **macro-environment** covers the Chapter 2 forces (economic, demographic, social, political-legal, technological, natural resources). The **microenvironment** covers competitors, suppliers, marketing intermediaries, the public, the company, and customers (Chapter 3).
- The **five-step purchase decision process**: problem recognition → information search → alternative evaluation (using evaluative criteria and rules of thumb) → purchase decision → post-purchase behaviour. **Post-purchase dissonance** (buyer’s remorse) is most likely for expensive, infrequent purchases; firms reduce it with guarantees, warranties, follow-up, and by setting expectations they can beat.
- Situational influences: physical surroundings and **atmospherics** (layout, music, lighting, temperature, smell), weather, crowding and herd behaviour, the social situation, time, the reason for buying, and mood.
- Psychological influences: **motivation** (Maslow’s hierarchy: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualization), **perception** (selective exposure, attention, retention, and distortion; shock vs. subliminal advertising), **learning** (operant conditioning through rewards; classical conditioning through association), **attitudes** (enduring and hard to change), and **lifestyle** (activities, interests, and opinions; psychographics; the VALS framework).
- Socio-cultural influences: personality (the Big Five: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism), **self-concept** and ideal self, gender, age and stage of life, **culture** (the broadest influence), subcultures, **reference groups** (aspirational or dissociative), **opinion leaders**, and family (one of the most important influences).
- The situation analysis feeds a **SWOT analysis**. Strengths and weaknesses are internal and somewhat controllable; opportunities and threats are external and largely uncontrollable. Quick test: take the company out of the picture. If the factor still exists (a weak economy), it is external.

## 10.4 Step 2: Market-Product Focus & Goal Setting

- Marketing **objectives** are end results to achieve in a set time; they should be realistic and measurable and consistent with corporate goals (“increase Gatorade’s market share by 4 percent by year-end”).
- The **Ansoff Matrix** links objectives to strategies. **Market penetration**: sell more existing products to existing customers (coupons, multi-pack discounts, Campbell’s recipes). **Product development**: new or improved products for existing customers (Mountain Dew Voltage). **Market development**: existing products in new markets, such as new age groups, regions, or countries. Exporting is the least risky, least costly way to enter foreign markets, but gives the least control.
- Segmentation choices: **mass marketing** (one offering for all; efficient but vulnerable), **multisegment marketing** (different offerings for different segments, like Marriott’s Courtyard, Ritz-Carlton, and ExecuStay brands; lets customers trade up or down), **concentrated marketing** (one select group; risky, as auto-parts makers learned in 2008), **niche marketing** (an even smaller group; Hohner holds 85% of harmonicas), and **micro-targeting** (narrowcasting with detailed personal data, which raises privacy concerns).
- An attractive target market is large enough to be profitable, growing, not swamped by competitors, accessible, within the company’s resources, and a fit with its mission (TerraCycle wouldn’t run a coal plant).
- **Positioning** is how consumers perceive a product relative to competitors. A **perceptual map** plots products on two criteria (such as price and quality) to find open space. Taglines support positioning (Wendy’s “It’s better than fast food”). **Repositioning** moves a product to a new place in consumers’ minds (Clayton’s i-house; Porsche’s longer Panamera for chauffeured buyers in China).

## 10.5 Step 3: The Marketing Mix (4 P’s)

- The **marketing mix** is product, price, place, and promotion, aimed at the chosen target market.
- **Marketing research** is collecting and analyzing data relevant to a specific marketing situation. Start with **secondary data** (already collected, such as sales reports or Statistics Canada), then gather **primary data** (new information) by surveys, personal interviews, or **focus groups** of 6 to 10 people led by a moderator. Wow Wee used focus groups and interviews for Robosapien.
- A **brand** is a word, letter, sound, or symbol that sets a product apart; a **trademark** legally protects it (registered with the Canadian Intellectual Property Office). Branding strategies: **private branding** (made by one firm, sold under a retailer’s name, like Great Value), **generic branding** (only a description of contents), and **manufacturer branding** (multi-product: one brand for everything; multi-branding: different brands for different segments, like Volkswagen, Audi, and Lamborghini). Packaging and labelling attract attention and inform (Robosapien’s clear package; 100 Calorie Packs).
- **Product life cycle**: **introduction** (high marketing costs, low profits), **growth** (rising sales and profits, competitors enter), **maturity** (sales level off, longest stage, repeat buyers, profits fall), **decline** (preferences or technology change). Clear Pepsi went from introduction to decline fast; Diet Coke has stayed mature.
- Pricing objectives: a targeted **return on investment** (\$100,000 invested at a 10% target ROI means \$10,000 profit), maximizing profits, maximizing sales (short-term cash), or maximizing market share. In introduction, firms choose **penetration pricing** (low price to win many triers) or **skimming** (high price to recoup costs from early adopters). Maturity risks price wars. In decline, firms **harvest** (cut costs gradually) or **divest** (drop or sell the product).
- Place: supply chain management and channel partners. **Wholesalers** buy in bulk and “break bulk” for retailers; merchant wholesalers take title (full-service or limited-service, including drop shippers, who take title but not possession, and rack jobbers). **Brokers/agents** don’t take title and earn commissions. **Retailers** include supermarkets, convenience stores, specialty stores and **category killers**, department and discount stores, superstores, warehouse clubs (membership fee), off-price and outlet stores, online and used retailers, **pop-up stores**, and nonstore retailing. Manufacturers may pay **slotting fees** for shelf space.
- The **promotion mix**: **advertising** (paid, non-personal; about 5,000 messages a day versus 500 in the 1970s; still the most common), **personal selling** (one-on-one; big-ticket items), **sales promotion** (coupons, BOGO, samples, displays, trade shows), and **publicity and public relations** (free coverage, like Beats headphones on the 2008 U.S. Olympic basketball team). A **loss leader** is priced below its minimum profit margin to draw buyers to other items.
- Promotion changes over the life cycle: build awareness and trial in introduction, stress benefits versus competitors in growth, promote heavily and extend the stage in maturity by changing the target market, product, or strategy (100 Calorie Packs topped \$200 million), and cut promotion and outlets in decline.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Marketing** | The activity, institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, partners, and society. |
| **Marketing concept** | Satisfying customer needs while meeting organizational goals such as profit and growth. |
| **Strategic planning** | A process that helps an organization allocate resources to capitalize on opportunities in the marketplace. |
| **Situation analysis** | Analyzing the internal and external environments and consumer behaviour before deciding on marketing actions. |
| **Microenvironment** | Competitors, suppliers, marketing intermediaries, the public, the company, and customers. |
| **Post-purchase dissonance** | Buyer’s remorse felt when a product doesn’t meet expectations, most common for expensive, infrequent purchases. |
| **Evaluative criteria** | The product characteristics a consumer uses to compare alternatives, such as price and size. |
| **Atmospherics** | Physical factors a firm controls, like layout, music, lighting, temperature, and smell, that influence shoppers. |
| **Maslow’s hierarchy of needs** | A theory that people satisfy basic needs (food, safety) before higher needs (social, esteem, self-actualization). |
| **Selective attention** | Filtering out information based on how relevant it is to you. |
| **Selective retention** | Forgetting information, even relevant information, often because it contradicts your beliefs. |
| **Selective distortion** | Misinterpreting the intended message of an ad or communication. |
| **Operant conditioning** | Learning through repeated behaviour that has positive or negative consequences, such as rewards for purchases. |
| **Classical conditioning** | Learning by associating a conditioned stimulus with an unconditioned stimulus to get a response. |
| **Psychographics** | Combining lifestyle and personality traits with attitudes, activities, and values to group similar consumers. |
| **Self-concept** | How you see yourself; people buy products to move closer to their ideal self. |
| **Culture** | The shared beliefs, customs, behaviours, and attitudes of a society; the broadest influence on consumer behaviour. |
| **Subculture** | A group within a culture that shares something, such as interests, religion, ethnicity, or location. |
| **Reference group** | A group a consumer identifies with or wants to join (aspirational) or avoids (dissociative). |
| **Opinion leader** | A person with expertise whose views others seek before buying. |
| **Ansoff Matrix** | A grid of product and market strategies: market penetration, product development, market development, and more. |
| **Market penetration** | Selling more existing products to existing customers. |
| **Product development** | Creating new or improved products for existing customers. |
| **Market development** | Entering new markets with existing products. |
| **Multisegment marketing** | Tailoring different offerings to different market segments. |
| **Concentrated marketing** | Targeting one very select group of customers. |
| **Niche marketing** | Targeting an even more select group, aiming to be a big fish in a small pond. |
| **Micro-targeting** | Isolating very narrow markets using detailed personal data; also called narrowcasting. |
| **Positioning** | How consumers perceive a product relative to competing products. |
| **Perceptual map** | A two-dimensional graph showing where a product stands relative to competitors on criteria buyers care about. |
| **Repositioning** | An effort to move a product to a different place in consumers’ minds. |
| **Marketing mix** | The 4 P’s: product, price, place, and promotion. |
| **Marketing research** | Collecting and analyzing data relevant to a specific marketing situation. |
| **Secondary data** | Information already collected, by the company or others, that relates to the target market. |
| **Primary data** | Newly collected information that addresses specific questions. |
| **Focus group** | A group of about six to ten people led by a moderator who asks about a product or strategy. |
| **Brand** | A word, letter, sound, or symbol that differentiates a product from similar products. |
| **Trademark** | A symbol, word, or words legally registered or established by use as representing a company or product. |
| **Private branding** | Making a product that a retailer resells under its own name. |
| **Generic branding** | Selling a product with no branding beyond a description of its contents. |
| **Multi-branding** | Using different brand names for different products aimed at different segments. |
| **Product life cycle** | The stages a product moves through: introduction, growth, maturity, and decline. |
| **Penetration pricing** | Setting a low initial price to get many customers to try a new product. |
| **Skimming** | Setting a high initial price to recoup development costs quickly from less price-sensitive buyers. |
| **Harvesting** | Gradually reducing all costs spent on a declining product to raise its profit until inventory runs out. |
| **Divesting** | Dropping a product from the company’s offerings or selling it to another firm. |
| **Breaking bulk** | Buying large quantities and dividing them into smaller units that retailers want. |
| **Drop shipper** | A wholesaler that takes title to goods but never takes possession; the producer ships directly. |
| **Broker (agent)** | An intermediary who negotiates sales for a commission but never takes title to the goods. |
| **Category killer** | A giant specialty store that dominates its product category, such as PetSmart or Best Buy. |
| **Slotting fee** | An allowance a manufacturer pays to secure space on store shelves. |
| **Promotion mix** | The ways a firm communicates with customers: advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, and publicity. |
| **Advertising** | Paid, non-personal communication designed to create awareness of a product or company. |
| **Sales promotion** | Incentives to buy, such as coupons, samples, displays, trade shows, and contests. |
| **Loss leader** | A product priced below its minimum profit margin to draw customers who buy other, more profitable items. |

### Multiple choice

**1. Which best describes the marketing concept?**

A. Advertising more than competitors  
B. Selling as much as possible regardless of need  
C. Producing goods as cheaply as possible  
D. Satisfying customer needs while meeting organizational goals  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Satisfying customer needs while meeting organizational goals.** Profit still matters; the customer comes first.

**2. Which is NOT one of the first three steps of the strategic marketing process covered in the course?**

A. Conducting a situation analysis  
B. Developing the marketing strategy  
C. Setting marketing objectives  
D. Hiring a sales force  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Hiring a sales force.** Step 4, implementing tactics, isn’t covered in this course.

**3. What is the correct order of the five-step purchase decision process?**

A. Problem recognition, purchase decision, information search, alternative evaluation, post-purchase behaviour  
B. Information search, problem recognition, purchase decision, alternative evaluation, post-purchase behaviour  
C. Problem recognition, information search, alternative evaluation, purchase decision, post-purchase behaviour  
D. Alternative evaluation, problem recognition, information search, post-purchase behaviour, purchase decision  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Problem recognition, information search, alternative evaluation, purchase decision, post-purchase behaviour.** Each stage builds on the one before.

**4. Post-purchase dissonance is most likely after buying:**

A. An expensive item purchased infrequently  
B. A pack of gum  
C. A loaf of bread  
D. A newspaper  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. An expensive item purchased infrequently.** Firms reduce it with warranties, guarantees, and follow-up.

**5. A restaurant says your table will be ready in 30 minutes and seats you in 15. Why?**

A. To set expectations it can beat, increasing satisfaction  
B. To reduce the menu  
C. To create herd behaviour  
D. To use skimming pricing  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. To set expectations it can beat, increasing satisfaction.** Beating expectations reduces dissatisfaction.

**6. Store music, lighting, temperature, and smells are called:**

A. Evaluative criteria  
B. Psychographics  
C. Slotting fees  
D. Atmospherics  

??? success "Show answer"
    **D. Atmospherics.** Firms control these physical factors.

**7. According to Maslow, which needs must be met first?**

A. Esteem  
B. Social belonging  
C. Physiological needs such as food, water, and sleep  
D. Self-actualization  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Physiological needs such as food, water, and sleep.** Higher-level needs come after basic needs.

**8. Forgetting an anti-smoking ad because it contradicts your behaviour is an example of:**

A. Classical conditioning  
B. Selective retention  
C. Selective exposure  
D. Selective distortion  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Selective retention.** People forget information that conflicts with their beliefs.

**9. Getting a free sandwich after buying ten is an example of:**

A. Positioning  
B. Operant conditioning  
C. Classical conditioning  
D. Selective attention  

??? success "Show answer"
    **B. Operant conditioning.** Rewards encourage repeat behaviour.

**10. Which is considered the broadest influence on consumer behaviour?**

A. Weather  
B. Mood  
C. Culture  
D. Store layout  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Culture.** Culture is a handed-down way of life.

**11. Nike hiring Michael Jordan appeals to consumers’ desire to belong to an:**

A. Micro-target  
B. Dissociative group  
C. Aspirational reference group  
D. Subculture of opinion leaders  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Aspirational reference group.** People want to be like the group.

**12. How can you tell if a SWOT factor is external?**

A. If it involves money, it’s external  
B. If it’s controllable, it’s external  
C. Remove the company; if the factor still exists, it’s external  
D. If it’s a strength, it’s external  

??? success "Show answer"
    **C. Remove the company; if the factor still exists, it’s external.** A weak economy exists with or without PepsiCo.

**13. Frito-Lay sends coupons to get current customers to buy more snacks. On the Ansoff Matrix this is:**

A. Market penetration  
B. Product development  
C. Market development  
D. Divesting  

??? success "Show answer"
    **A. Market penetration.** Existing products, existing customers.

A. Harvesting  
B. Market development  
C. Market penetration

    **D. Product development.** New products for existing customers.

**15. Coffee-maker brands target coffee-shop customers who have never brewed premium coffee at home. This is:**

A. Product development  
B. Market development

D. Market penetration

**16. Which way of entering a foreign market is least risky but gives the least control?**

B. Building a factory abroad

A. Mass marketing

**18. Hohner holds 85% of the harmonica market. This is an example of:**

A. Penetration pricing  
B. Niche marketing

**19. Which is NOT a characteristic of an attractive target market?**

A. Multi-branding

**24. Which product life cycle stage usually lasts longest?**

A. Introduction  
B. Growth  
C. Maturity

**25. Sales rise, competitors enter quickly, and profits increase in the:**

A. Maturity stage  
B. Growth stage

### True or false

**1. Marketing is only about advertising and selling.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** It includes everything done to satisfy customer needs, from product design to after-sale relationships.

**2. Every job in a business, even the ticket seller’s, can be part of marketing.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** Marketing is a team effort.

**3. Tropicana returned to its familiar packaging after spending \$35 million on a redesign customers disliked.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** It’s an example of learning from past strategies.

**4. Strengths and weaknesses are external factors in a SWOT analysis.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** They’re internal and somewhat controllable.

**5. Consumers are more likely to feel buyer’s remorse after expensive, infrequent purchases.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** That’s why big-ticket items come with warranties and follow-up.

**6. Concentrated marketing is low-risk because the company targets many segments.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** It’s risky because all the eggs are in one basket.

**7. Micro-targeting raises ethical and privacy concerns.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** It relies on detailed personal data.

**8. Primary data is information already collected by someone else.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** That’s secondary data; primary data is newly collected.

**9. A focus group usually involves about six to ten people and a moderator.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** Wow Wee used focus groups for Robosapien.

**10. Profits are usually highest in the introduction stage of the product life cycle.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** Profits are often low because of development and launch costs.

**11. Clear Pepsi moved from introduction to decline very quickly.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** Not every product goes through every stage.

**12. Maximizing sales is usually a long-term pricing objective.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** It’s typically short-term, used to raise cash quickly.

**13. Wholesalers “break bulk” into smaller units for retailers.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** They resell goods without transforming them.

**14. Brokers take title to the products they sell.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** Brokers negotiate sales but never take title.

**15. A loss leader must always be priced below cost.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **False.** It’s priced below its minimum profit margin, not necessarily below cost.

**16. The average consumer sees far more ads per day now than in the 1970s.**

??? success "Show answer"
    **True.** About 5,000 a day now, versus about 500 then.
